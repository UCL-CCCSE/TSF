1.1
Both mariners and aristocrats suffering in the storm
“What cares these roarers for the name of king?”
All humanity is vulnerable in the face of extreme weather

Prospero’s freeing of Ariel
“I will rend an oak / And peg thee in his knotty entrails”
Human power is expressed through control of natural environment

Caliban’s intimate knowledge of place… and Prospero’s dependency on that knowledge
“The fresh springs, brine pits, barren place and fertile”

“We cannot miss him; he does make our fire”
Humans have material needs which must be met from resources of the natural environment
2.1
Contrasting perspectives of the island environment
“The air breathes upon us here most sweetly”
Landscapes/environments are often constructed through human perspectives

Caliban’s log-carrying

Deforestation is frequently depicted as oppressive manual labour
2.2
Caliban’s detailed natural knowledge
“let me bring thee where crabs grow”
Intimate knowledge of the land confers belonging and a kind of power
3.1
Ferdinand bearing logs
“I must remove / some thousands of these logs”
Deforestation is figured as excessive and expressive of patriarchal authority
4.1
The masque
“thy rich leas / Of wheat, rye, barley”
The masque celebrates natural fertility while being a mode of courtly consumption
5.1
Prospero’s power over nature
“I have bedimmed / The noontide sun, called forth the mutinous winds”
Prospero is a symbol of anthropocentric power over the weather and natural beings… and its renunciation
